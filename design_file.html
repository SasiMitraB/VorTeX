Non-lognormal density probability distribution and turbulent Mach numbers in the local Interstellar Medium from three-dimensional data

Sasi M. Behara1,2* and Amit Seta1†
¹ Research School of Astronomy and Astrophysics, Australian National University, Canberra, ACT 2611, Australia
² Department of Physics, Indian Institute of Science Education and Research, Tirupati 517619, India

Abstract

The probability distribution function (PDF) of gas density is a key tool used to study turbulence and star formation in the interstellar medium (ISM), and is typically modelled as a lognormal distribution. Observational studies have been limited to two-dimensional column-density maps, which fail to capture the intermittency, which are key to alternative, non-lognormal models such as the Castaing-Hopkins Model. We use the parsec-scale three-dimensional dust map of the local ISM (≤ 1250 pc) from Edenhofer et al. (2024) to measure, for the first time the volumetric density PDF of individual molecular clouds directly in 3D.

Key words: turbulence — ISM: clouds — stars: formation — methods: statistical

1 Introduction

The interstellar medium (ISM) in galaxies is the space between stars and consists of thermal gas, dust, radiation, turbulence, magnetic fields, and cosmic rays. The composition and evolution of the ISM play an important role in star formation and galaxy evolution (Ferrière 2001). The formation of stars is due to a complex interplay between gravity, turbulence, magnetic fields, and stellar feedback (Mac Low & Klessen 2004; Krumholz & McKee 2005; Federrath & Klessen 2012).

Supersonic turbulence plays a crucial role in the star formation process. On one hand, kinetic energy from turbulence and gas pressure provides support against gravitational collapse. On the other hand, supersonic turbulence compresses the gas into local regions of overdense gas, which can collapse under self-gravity.

2 Theoretical Models

Turbulence in the ISM is driven by physical processes at a range of scales, from stellar jets to supernova explosions at kpc scales. These drivers create density fluctuations that can be mathematically parameterized by density PDFs:

p(s) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_s^2}} \exp\left[-\frac{(s-s_0)^2}{2\sigma_s^2}\right]

where s = ln(ρ/ρ₀) is the logarithmic density contrast and σₛ is related to the sonic Mach number $\mathcal{M}$.
